# PROJECT SYNAPSE

# Document 10 — Interaction Language & Human Experience System

Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Interaction Language defines how visitors communicate with SYNAPSE.

Unlike traditional websites where interactions trigger interface changes, SYNAPSE treats every interaction as a conversation between the visitor and the world.

Every movement, click, hover, scroll, or pause influences the universe.

The world is always listening.

---

# Philosophy

Interaction is not input.

Interaction is communication.

The visitor never "uses" the website.

The visitor forms a relationship with it.

Every response should acknowledge curiosity.

Never simply execute commands.

---

# Interaction Principles

## Principle 1

The world notices you.

Nothing happens until the visitor arrives.

Once they arrive,

the universe becomes aware.

Particles begin moving.

Light subtly shifts.

Fog responds.

Everything acknowledges presence.

---

## Principle 2

Curiosity is rewarded.

Every meaningful interaction should reveal something.

Not necessarily immediately.

But eventually.

Exploration always feels worthwhile.

---

## Principle 3

Nothing feels mechanical.

No buttons snapping.

No instant hover effects.

No rigid UI.

Everything eases.

Everything breathes.

Everything responds naturally.

---

## Principle 4

The world is patient.

If a visitor pauses,

the experience doesn't punish them.

Instead,

the universe continues living quietly.

Idle visitors still experience subtle motion.

---

## Principle 5

Interaction has consequences.

Clicking a project might awaken nearby particles.

Talking to the AI might change the sky.

Discovering a hidden object may permanently unlock a new constellation.

Interactions leave memories.

# Cursor Behaviour

The cursor is part of the world.

It is not an operating system pointer.

The cursor behaves like a small source of energy.

Nearby particles drift toward it.

Soft light follows it.

Fog subtly parts.

Nothing aggressively chases it.

---

# Hover Language

Hover is curiosity.

Not selection.

Hovering never immediately activates objects.

Instead:

Object notices visitor.

↓

Nearby particles respond.

↓

Lighting shifts.

↓

Object awakens.

↓

Information gradually reveals itself.

Hover feels like introducing yourself.

---

# Focus Language

Focus represents intention.

When an object gains focus:

Background distractions soften.

Ambient particles redirect.

Lighting emphasizes the subject.

Camera subtly acknowledges importance.

Focus should feel respectful.

Never dramatic.

---

# Click Language

Click means:

"I want to know more."

It should never:

Teleport

Instantly open panels

Explode information

Instead:

The object responds.

It opens itself naturally.

The world supports the interaction.

The visitor remains immersed.

---

# Scroll Language

Scroll means:

"Continue the story."

Never:

Next page.

Never:

Move content.

Always:

Advance the universe.

---

# Drag Language

Dragging should exist only when it provides meaningful exploration.

Examples:

Rotating project artifacts.

Manipulating architecture diagrams.

Inspecting 3D models.

Never drag purely for novelty.

---

# Pause Language

Pausing is an interaction.

If the visitor stops:

The universe breathes.

Particles regroup.

AI occasionally looks toward the visitor.

Ambient systems continue living.

The experience never appears frozen.

---

# AI Interaction

Digital Jaasim is not separate from the world.

Talking to the AI influences:

Lighting.

Camera.

Project activation.

Particle flow.

Constellations.

The AI feels embedded in the universe.

---

# Hidden Interaction System

The world rewards exploration.

Examples:

Looking at a constellation long enough reveals its meaning.

Hovering the MJ monogram awakens hidden dialogue.

Typing "hello" in unexpected places unlocks a conversation.

Certain project sequences reveal bonus content.

Nothing is announced.

Everything is discovered.

Avoid:

Hover spam.

Instant UI panels.

Competing focus states.

Tiny interaction targets.

Invisible affordances.

Repeated interaction patterns.

Interactions with no feedback.

Cursor treated as a normal pointer.

Interactions that break immersion.
